# External Review Simulation — "Exact Local Output Perturbation in KV-Cache Eviction"

Reviewed as a skeptical ML/transformer-efficiency reviewer, reading the PDF cold (Part I of this round's brief) before consulting source, claim ledger, or result files.

## First-pass reading (before touching the repo)

**What I think the paper claims (by end of page 2):** the authors have an exact formula for how much a single attention head's output changes when one KV-cache token is evicted and the rest renormalized; they use it to derive an exact condition for when this "true" ranking of eviction damage disagrees with the cheaper attention-mass heuristic; they show this disagreement happens a few percent of the time and almost always changes what gets evicted and produces a measurable output difference; but — and this is clearly the actual point of the paper — the token that looks better by this exact local measure is *not* reliably the token that turns out better once the rest of the network has processed the eviction, roughly a third of the time it flips, and four different attempts to explain *when* it flips all fail or only partially succeed. **The main contribution is legible by the end of the abstract**, which is unusual and appreciated — most mechanistic-interpretability-adjacent papers bury the actual finding on page 6.

**Theorem vs. computation vs. interpretation:** cleanly separated. The paper is explicit, in its own prose, about which of its four numbered statements is which, and even pre-empts my "is this just algebra?" objection in the Discussion (more below).

**What I'd challenge on a first pass, before checking anything:** (1) is the "exact identity" actually new, given how simple it looks — a paper doesn't usually lead with an identity this clean unless either nobody thought to write it down, or somebody already did; (2) the architectures are small and old (160M/124M) — does any of this survive at scale; (3) "measurable KL" is doing a lot of work as the paper's operational notion of "consequence" — is it possible that everything downstream of statement (3) is real but practically negligible.

## Strengths

- The central distinction (exact local perturbation $\neq$ guaranteed downstream consequence) is maintained consistently from abstract through conclusion — I could not find a place where it quietly collapses.
- The prior-art correction (KeepKV) is stated in the abstract, the introduction, and worked out algebraically term-by-term in §4.2, rather than buried in related work. That is exactly the right way to handle a scooped result, and it is rare to see a paper volunteer this so plainly.
- The falsification sequence (DVG$\to$DPH$\to$FLH$\to$SDPH) is a genuinely well-constructed negative-result narrative: each stage is motivated by the previous stage's failure, each stage pre-declares its own effect-size thresholds rather than chasing p-values, and the one positive causal finding (SDPH) is explicitly and repeatedly flagged as *not* an explanation for the phenomenon it was designed to explain. This is a hard discipline to maintain and the paper maintains it.
- Cluster-aware uncertainty is the correct statistical response to the paper's own sampling design, the clustering unit is well-motivated (shared forward pass, not merely the depth/winner sampling stratum), and the paired FLH-vs-DPH comparison correctly resamples the same clusters jointly rather than computing two independent bootstraps and subtracting.
- Figures are honest: axes start at zero where a truncated axis would have flattered the argument, Figure 3 is explicit that four hand-selected trajectories are not distributional evidence, and Figure 4 states the SDPH "confirmed to exist / falsified as explanation" distinction as plainly as the prose does.

## Major concerns

1. **Scope is two small, dated architectures.** Pythia-160M and GPT-2-124M are both far below current deployed scale, and both predate the KV-cache-eviction literature this paper positions itself against (which mostly evaluates on 3B-70B instruction-tuned models). The paper is honest about this in Limitations, but it is the single biggest thing standing between this paper and broader relevance: nothing here has been shown to survive at a scale anyone would actually deploy eviction on.
2. **No comparison to task-level consequence.** "Measurable KL divergence above a numerical noise floor" is a well-justified, honestly-scoped notion of "consequence" for the paper's own narrow question, but it is not evidence of practical importance, and the paper says so explicitly. A reader who came in hoping to learn whether any of this matters for downstream task accuracy will leave without an answer — which is a legitimate scoping choice, but it caps how much this paper can claim to matter outside mechanistic interpretability.
3. **The theoretical core is thin, and the paper knows it.** Theorem 5.1's proof is two lines of algebra. The paper's own new Discussion paragraph ("On the simplicity of Theorem 2's proof") concedes this directly and relocates the contribution to "making a previously case-by-case empirical question exact and checkable at scale." I find this a *credible* defense, but it is a defense, not a rebuttal — a reviewer who weights theoretical depth heavily will not be fully satisfied by it, and I would not have been surprised if this paragraph were absent, since owning the objection this explicitly is a judgment call some author teams would avoid making in their own manuscript.

## Minor concerns

- The DVG negative result is scoped to "the eight tested cosine/norm features," which is appropriately narrow language, but no explicit sentence rules out a differently-featurized directional-geometry signal existing; a one-line caveat would close this off entirely.
- No confidence interval is reported for the DVG AUC point estimates (0.5496 / 0.5223) in the main text, only for the DPH/FLH/SDPH cluster-bootstrap quantities.
- SDPH's causal existence claim rests on $n=24$ hand-stratified cases. The wording ("confirmed... causally demonstrated") is defensible as an *existence* claim validated by intervention with exact (0.0) numerical agreement, not a *prevalence* claim, and the paper does not overreach into the latter — but a reviewer will still want a larger swap-intervention audit before fully trusting the reported flip/stable asymmetry direction.
- GPT-2 was loaded without a pinned revision (unlike Pythia); the manuscript now discloses this honestly rather than hiding it, but it is a real asymmetry in the reproducibility guarantee between the two architectures.
- Exact library versions (torch/transformers/numpy) are not stated in the main text (they exist in the repository's stored run provenance and `requirements-lock.txt`, but are not surfaced in the paper).

## Novelty assessment

Tested directly against the paper's own claims:

1. **Is the singleton deletion identity already in KeepKV?** Yes — I re-derived both forms by hand and they are term-for-term identical, including sign. The manuscript says so, unprompted, in the abstract, introduction, and §4.2 explicitly ("we state plainly that the singleton exact-perturbation identity is not a novel contribution of this paper"). **This objection is fully defused by the paper's own wording — a reviewer cannot use it as a rejection ground, because the paper got there first.**
2. **Is the block-deletion formula a direct generalization?** Yes, and the paper says so ("a direct linear extension... not presented as a separate contribution"). No issue.
3. **Is $d_i$ as a ranking score distinguishable from prior work?** Partially. Structurally it sits in the same family as CriticalKV's bound and the Fixed-Contract Diagnostic's centroid-distance score (both cited, both 2025/2026), and the paper is honest that these are "structurally related... to our knowledge" rather than claiming a clean gap. $d_i$ itself is not presented as a standalone contribution in the paper's own Contributions list — it is correctly framed as infrastructure for the $\Gamma$ boundary, which is the right call and pre-empts a "this is just a trivial corollary" objection.
4. **Is $\Gamma$ the cleanest genuinely novel contribution?** Yes, on the evidence available to me (I cannot independently re-run the paper's literature search, but the related-work section's coverage of the closest KV-cache-eviction papers is credible and specific enough to trust as a good-faith search, not a token related-work section).
5. **Is $\Gamma$ algebraically trivial, and does that sink the paper?** The proof is trivial. It does not sink the paper, because the paper does not lean on proof depth — it leans on (a) the boundary condition making a previously-informal comparison exact and checkable at scale (482,787 verification checks across two architectures with zero mismatches) and (b) the empirical/causal program built on top of it. I find that a legitimate basis for a paper, though a reviewer who wants "hard math" will be unimpressed regardless of how the empirical program turns out — that reviewer is evaluating the wrong axis for what this paper is trying to be, but it is a defensible axis to prioritize.

**Overall:** the paper cannot be rejected on novelty grounds as such — every genuinely non-novel piece is disclosed by the authors themselves, which removes the most common way a novelty objection succeeds (the reviewer discovering it independently, embarrassing the authors). The remaining novelty risk is a matter of *taste* (is a simple theorem plus a careful empirical program "enough"), not a matter of an undisclosed prior-art collision.

## Statistical assessment

I looked specifically for the failure mode named in this round's brief: pooled DPH/FLH point estimates being described as if they were natural population prevalence rather than estimates within a depth-tertile$\times$final-winner stratified design. **I did not find this failure anywhere in the current draft.** The word "prevalence" is reserved exclusively for the full-grid inversion-prevalence numbers (3.55%/5.38%, and the disclosed-as-non-headline 6.62% pilot figure); every mention of the DPH/FLH flip/immediate/unresolved rates is either directly attached to "of the stratified sample" / "of 250 such cases" language, or appears in a table whose immediately preceding paragraph (§7.2) states the distinction explicitly: "these rates should not be read as raw, unweighted population prevalence among all inversion pairs, only as estimates within the sampled design." This exact sentence, plus its restatement in Limitations, is doing real work and should stay verbatim.

Other statistical checks:
- Cluster-bootstrap unit (architecture$\times$prompt$\times$layer$\times$head) is well-motivated and explicitly distinguished from the sampling stratum used for case *selection* — this distinction is stated correctly and is not trivial to get right.
- Effective cluster counts are reported honestly, including the Pythia-side weakness (34 clusters, 9 singleton) — the paper does not hide behind $n=250$ pairs when the real uncertainty-bearing unit is much smaller.
- The Pythia-vs-GPT-2 architecture-difference interval crosses zero and the paper says, plainly, that no architecture difference is established — it does not quietly promote the point estimate.
- The FLH-vs-DPH comparison is genuinely paired (same resampled clusters applied to both within each replicate), which is the statistically correct way to estimate that difference given the two analyses share every case.
- The local-vs-mass subgroup asymmetry is reported with its own effective cluster counts (89/93) and is explicitly labeled "an empirical asymmetry requiring explanation, not a mechanism" — appropriately hedged.
- No family-wise (Bonferroni-style) correction is applied across DVG/DPH/FLH/SDPH, and the paper defends this choice explicitly (directed falsification narrative, pre-declared effect sizes, disclosed sample reuse) rather than silently omitting it. I find this defense adequate, though a frequentist purist could still disagree.

## Reproducibility assessment

After this round's addition of a "Reproducibility" paragraph (§5), a reviewer cloning the repository can determine: exact HF model identifiers for both architectures, Pythia's pinned git revision (12-char prefix of a stored 40-char SHA — honestly labeled as a prefix rather than presented as if it were the whole hash), the fact that GPT-2 was *not* revision-pinned (disclosed, not concealed), the fixed seed ($20{,}260{,}910$) used for every stratified draw, resample, and SDPH case selection in the paper, the 1:1 naming correspondence between every cited `results/` directory and its generating `experiments/*.py` script, the KL convention and measurable-threshold definitions (stated inline where first used, in §6 and §7), the cluster-bootstrap method (§7.2), and the Figure 3 case-selection rule (stated in-prose and in the figure caption, with a full log in a companion provenance file). The one item this round could not add without re-running anything is exact library-version pinning in the main text (it exists in the repository but is not surfaced in the paper) — a minor, easily fixed gap in a later round, not a blocker now.

## Recommendation

**Weak Accept.**

This is a careful, honest, methodologically disciplined paper whose empirical/causal program is considerably stronger than its theoretical core, and which is unusually forthcoming about both facts. Nothing in this review found an error, an unresolved overclaim, or an undisclosed novelty collision — every weakness I identified is either already disclosed by the paper itself or is a matter of scope (small architectures, no task-level evaluation) rather than a matter of correctness. That combination does not typically earn a *Strong* Accept, because the theoretical contribution alone would not carry the paper, and the practical stakes (does this matter for real deployed eviction?) are explicitly left open — but it comfortably clears Accept-level rigor and honesty, and I would not fight to keep it out.

### The single biggest factor determining acceptance

**Whether the venue and reviewer pool value a rigorously-executed negative/falsification result and a modest-but-honestly-framed theoretical core over a flashier positive result or a practical algorithmic payoff.** This paper explicitly is not a new eviction algorithm, not a benchmark win, and not a deep theorem — it is a precise, well-instrumented account of where a clean local theory stops predicting global behavior, delivered with genuine statistical and reproducibility discipline. In a venue or review pool that rewards that kind of contribution (mechanistic interpretability, careful empirical ML methodology), this clears Accept comfortably. In a venue or review pool primarily interested in "does this make eviction better," the honest "we do not evaluate this" limitation will read as a bigger cost than the paper's rigor can offset, and the same paper could plausibly land at Borderline instead. The manuscript cannot control which reviewer pool it draws; that is the single largest source of variance in how this review would actually resolve.
